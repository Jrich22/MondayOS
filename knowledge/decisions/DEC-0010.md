These rules govern Cue App's Invite & RSVP increment (TASK-0023). They extend DEC-0008 (roadmap) and build on the delivered Event Planning foundation (TASK-0049, DOC-0015, DEC-0009). They do not authorize implementation.

1. MVP architecture

Build the complete invitation and RSVP journey as a browser-local product prototype within the existing React/Vite/localStorage architecture. Do not introduce a backend, production authentication, or an email provider in this increment. The guest-facing experience must be clearly documented as a prototype: it is not production-public or cross-device capable. A future decision will govern backend persistence, production public access, and real delivery.

2. Guest access

Use an opaque, per-invitation response link/token. Guests do not create accounts or sign in. Tokens must contain no PII and should follow Cue's existing signed-credential precedent (the client-side signed badge/QR token in lib/qr). Support revocation and rotation semantics in the product model.

3. Delivery

Support copying an invitation link and simulated campaign delivery through the existing Communications experience. Simulated delivery updates canonical invitation-delivery state (the existing guest communication flags). Do not integrate an external email or messaging provider.

4. Guests and companions

Keep plus-ones as an anonymous count for this increment, constrained by the invitation's allowance and event capacity. Named companions, households, and linked guest records are deferred.

5. RSVP responses and privacy

Allow confirmed, tentative, and declined responses, plus the permitted plus-one count. Support optional dietary and accessibility responses using the existing guest fields. Do not add custom RSVP questions in this increment. Dietary and accessibility information may be sensitive and is visible only within the current operator prototype; production privacy, retention, and access controls remain prerequisites for a production release.

6. Capacity and waitlist

Capacity remains a hard constraint separate from RSVP demand (per DEC-0009 §3). When accepting a response would exceed remaining capacity, place the invitation on the waitlist automatically if waitlisting is enabled; otherwise prevent confirmation and explain why. Waitlist promotion remains an explicit organizer decision — do not automatically promote guests.

7. Editing and withdrawal

Guests may revise or withdraw their response until the event begins, provided the invitation remains active. Withdrawal releases projected capacity but does not automatically promote a waitlisted guest.

8. Organizer monitoring and reminders

Add the response funnel and pending-response visibility to Event Detail, filling the existing Invite/RSVP lifecycle gap. Reuse existing Communications audiences and RSVP-reminder campaigns for manual reminders. Mission Control may surface existing actionable waitlist decisions but must not duplicate RSVP state.

9. Guest experience

The guest response surface is mobile-first, accessible, and concise. Reuse existing event presentation and theme data where available. Do not create a separate branding system.

10. Scope boundaries

This increment is RSVP, not registration or ticketing. Exclude payments, tickets, seating, named households, custom form builders, automated waitlist promotion, production delivery, production authentication, and backend infrastructure.

These rules must be used when MondayOS later translates the approved Invite & RSVP increment into engineering work.
